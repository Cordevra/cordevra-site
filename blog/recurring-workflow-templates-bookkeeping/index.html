Recurring workflow templates for a bookkeeping practice

Copy-ready templates for monthly bookkeeping, payroll, payables, quarterly and year-end work, and how to design templates your team will actually follow.

November 17, 2026 · 5 min read

Most of the work in a bookkeeping practice repeats. The same monthly close, the same payroll run, the same year-end scramble. Templates turn that repetition into an advantage: the steps are written once, every client gets the same quality, and new team members can follow the process from day one.

Below are the templates we would start a practice with. Treat them as drafts. The best template is the one that matches how your firm actually works, refined every time something goes wrong.

What makes a good workflow template

  • Steps, not paragraphs. Each step is one action someone can tick off.
  • Owners. Every step has a role: preparer, reviewer, client lead or the client.
  • Relative deadlines. "Five business days after period end" works for every month; "October 7" works once.
  • Client requests built in. If a step needs something from the client, the request is part of the template, so it goes out automatically.
  • A review step. Work that reaches the client should be checked by a second person.
  • A link to the procedure. Steps that need detail should point to your written SOP, not repeat it.

Template 1: Monthly bookkeeping

The core template for most clients. Deadlines are relative to period end.

  1. Day 1: Document requests go to the client: statements for each account, payroll reports, processor reports.
  2. Day 1 to 7: Categorize transactions as they come in; match deposits and payments.
  3. Day 7: Document deadline; reminders for anything outstanding.
  4. Day 8 to 10: Reconcile bank, card, loan and payroll liability accounts.
  5. Day 10: Send client questions as one list.
  6. Day 12: Record adjusting entries; post approved client answers.
  7. Day 13: Reviewer checks reconciliations and financial statements.
  8. Day 14: Close the period; deliver financials with a short summary.
  9. Day 15: Invoice for the month.

For the detail inside each step, see our month-end close checklist.

Template 2: Payroll cycle

Runs every pay period. Deadlines are relative to the pay date.

  1. Pay date minus 4: Request hours, new hires, terminations and changes from the client.
  2. Pay date minus 3: Enter payroll; check against the prior period for unusual changes.
  3. Pay date minus 2: Client approves the payroll preview.
  4. Pay date minus 2: Submit payroll.
  5. Pay date: Confirm payments and tax deposits went through.
  6. Pay date plus 1: Record the payroll entry in the books.

Add a quarterly sub-template for payroll tax filings and an annual one for year-end forms.

Template 3: Accounts payable

For clients where you run bill pay, usually weekly or twice a month.

  1. Collect and enter new bills; check for duplicates.
  2. Check vendor details against what is on file; any change to bank details gets verified by phone with a known contact before payment.
  3. Prepare the pay run.
  4. Client approves, holds or rejects each bill.
  5. Pay approved bills on the scheduled date.
  6. Record payments and file remittance details.

Collect a W-9 from each new vendor at the first bill rather than in January. Year-end 1099 work is much easier when it is already done.

Template 4: Accounts receivable and collections

  1. Send invoices on the agreed schedule.
  2. Apply incoming payments.
  3. Send reminders for invoices past due, on a fixed rhythm.
  4. Escalate long-overdue balances to the client with a recommendation.
  5. Review the AR aging with the client monthly.

Template 5: Quarterly review

  1. Review the quarter's financials against the prior year.
  2. Check sales tax filings and payroll tax filings for the quarter.
  3. Review estimated tax payments, if part of your scope.
  4. Check the client's volume against their pricing tier.
  5. Hold a short review call; note any changes to the business.
  6. Update the client's workflows if anything changed.

The volume check is easy to skip and expensive to forget. Our guide on pricing bookkeeping services explains why.

Template 6: Year-end

  1. Request year-end documents: December statements, loan balances, fixed asset purchases, inventory counts if relevant.
  2. Close December with extra review.
  3. Confirm W-9s are on file for every vendor who needs one; prepare 1099s.
  4. Prepare year-end adjusting entries.
  5. Produce the year-end package for the client and their tax preparer.
  6. Lock the year.

Template 7: Annual engagement renewal

  1. Review the client's scope, volume and profitability for the year.
  2. Decide on any price or scope change.
  3. Send the renewed engagement letter for signature.
  4. Update services, tiers and workflows to match.

Running renewals as a workflow means letters go out on schedule rather than whenever someone remembers.

Rolling templates out across your client list

Start with one template

Pick the monthly bookkeeping template, apply it to every client for two months and fix what breaks. Adding six templates at once makes it hard to tell which one is causing problems.

Allow variations, not exceptions

A client with inventory needs an extra step. Add it to that client's version of the template rather than relying on someone to remember. The goal is that nothing important lives only in a person's head.

Watch the "waiting on client" steps

Most delays sit in steps owned by the client. Track them separately so you can see whether a late close is a team problem or a client problem. Our guide to collecting documents without chasing covers this.

Review templates after each busy season

Every year-end teaches you something. Spend an hour afterward updating the templates while the lessons are fresh.

Signs your templates are working

  • A new team member can run a close by following the template.
  • You can answer "which clients are behind this month, and why?" in under a minute.
  • Closes finish around the same day each month.
  • Clients stop asking what you need from them, because the requests arrive on schedule.
