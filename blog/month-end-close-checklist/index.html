Month-end close checklist for small business clients

Our close checklist for small business clients, in the order we run it, plus the habits that keep the close from slipping into next month.

November 10, 2026 · 5 min read

A month-end close is the difference between "the books are probably fine" and "the books are done." For small business clients the close does not need to be elaborate, but it does need to be consistent: the same steps, in the same order, every month, with someone checking the work.

Below is the checklist we use for a typical small business: a handful of bank and card accounts, payroll, some bills and some invoices. Add steps for inventory, multiple entities or job costing when a client needs them.

Before the month ends: set up the close

  • Send document requests early. Request statements and reports as soon as the period ends, not when you sit down to reconcile. If requests go out automatically each month, even better.
  • Confirm the deadline. Know the date the client expects financials and work backward: when do you need documents, when must reconciliation finish, when is review?
  • Assign the preparer and reviewer. Even in a two-person firm, the person who reviews should not be the person who prepared.

Step 1: Collect the documents

  1. Bank statements for every account.
  2. Credit card statements for every card.
  3. Loan and line of credit statements.
  4. Payroll reports for each pay date in the period.
  5. Merchant processor and payment platform reports.
  6. Any new contracts, leases or large purchases the client made.

If documents tend to arrive late, our guide on collecting documents without chasing covers request design and reminders.

Step 2: Process the transactions

  • Categorize all bank and card transactions for the period.
  • Match deposits to invoices and payments to bills.
  • Record payroll entries and check they agree with the payroll reports.
  • Record merchant fees and processor payouts correctly, gross versus net.
  • Collect anything you cannot identify into a list of client questions. Do not guess.

Step 3: Reconcile

Reconcile every balance sheet account that has an outside source to agree with:

  • Every bank and credit card account, to the statement ending balance.
  • Loans, to the lender statement, with interest split correctly.
  • Payroll liabilities, to the payroll provider's reports.
  • Sales tax payable, to what was collected and filed.
  • Undeposited funds or clearing accounts, which should be close to zero or fully explained.

A reconciliation with old uncleared items is not finished. Investigate anything older than a month or two.

Step 4: Review accounts receivable and payable

  • Run the AR aging. Are old invoices really unpaid, or were payments recorded without being applied?
  • Run the AP aging. Are there duplicate bills, or bills that were paid outside the system?
  • Flag overdue customer balances to the client; collections often matter more to a small business than any report.

Step 5: Adjusting entries

Depending on the client and the basis of accounting, this may include:

  • Depreciation and amortization.
  • Prepaid expenses released to the period.
  • Accrued expenses for costs incurred but not yet billed.
  • Deferred revenue for amounts billed in advance.
  • Owner draws, contributions and reclassifications.

Keep a standing list of recurring adjustments for each client so nothing depends on memory.

Step 6: Ask the client, once

Send the client one organized list of questions, not a trickle of emails. For each item include the date, amount, description and your best guess. Clients answer faster when they only have to confirm or correct. Give them a clear deadline and a single place to answer.

When answers come back, review them before they change the books. "Personal" might mean a reimbursable expense; "equipment" might be a repair. Approve, then post.

Step 7: Review the financial statements

The reviewer looks at the result, not just the process:

  • Compare the profit and loss to the prior month and the same month last year. Investigate big swings.
  • Scan the balance sheet for negative balances, stale accounts and anything that looks odd.
  • Check that equity rolls forward correctly from the prior period.
  • Confirm all reconciliations are complete and signed off.
  • Confirm the uncategorized and suspense accounts are empty.

Step 8: Close the period

Lock the period in the accounting system so later edits do not change numbers the client has already seen. Record the close date and who reviewed it. If a change is needed later, it should be a deliberate decision, not an accident.

Step 9: Deliver and invoice

  • Send the client their financial statements with a short summary: what changed, what needs attention, and anything they should decide.
  • Invoice for the month's work, ideally with online payment so the client can pay right away.
  • Note anything that should change next month: a new account, a new vendor, a recurring question.

Habits that keep the close on time

Make it recurring, not remembered

The close should create itself each month with the same tasks and deadlines. Rebuilding it by hand is where steps get skipped. See our recurring workflow templates for a monthly template you can adapt.

Separate "waiting on us" from "waiting on the client"

When a close is late, you need to know why. Track client-owed items separately, with how long each has been open, so you can follow up on the right thing.

Review the checklist quarterly

Clients change. A checklist written for a client a year ago may miss a new loan or a new payment platform. A quick quarterly review keeps the close honest.

Printable summary

  1. Requests sent, deadline confirmed, preparer and reviewer assigned.
  2. All statements and reports received.
  3. Transactions categorized; unknowns listed as questions.
  4. Bank, card, loan, payroll and tax accounts reconciled.
  5. AR and AP agings reviewed.
  6. Adjusting entries recorded.
  7. Client questions sent once, answered and reviewed.
  8. Financial statements reviewed against prior periods.
  9. Period closed and locked.
  10. Financials delivered and the month invoiced.
